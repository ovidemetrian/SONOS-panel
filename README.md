## The Philosophy: The Final Ten Feet

After 30 years of designing and installing custom AV and smart-home systems, one truth remains: **the final ten feet is everything.**

You can engineer flawless audio hardware and a bulletproof network, but the final ten feet is defined entirely by the people on the sofa. The speakers, the lights, the TV and everything in the rack have to disappear so the experience can take over. If someone hesitates at the glass to figure out how to group the kitchen and the patio, the moment is gone.

True enjoyment happens when friction drops to zero. People don't want to manage their system. They want to live in it. The SONOS Panel is designed around that reality.

## Tap ’n Go

Finger down, result up. The whole panel follows one sequence:

### LOOK → KNOW → TAP → DONE

No hunting through menus. No remembering which room you picked three screens ago. No "system management" language. The house shows its state, and the obvious, physical-looking control does the job.

| You notice… | You tap… | Done |
|---|---|---|
| Sound you don't want | **HUSH** | The whole house goes quiet. |
| Music somewhere in the house | **WHAT'S PLAYING NOW** | You see where, at once. |
| The room you want | **its colour** | You're controlling it. |
| You want the whole house | **SONOS** | Back to every room. |
| You're lost or confused | **BACK TO SQUARE ONE** | A known, clean state, with UNDO. |

> **If you have to stop and figure out the interface before you can control the sound, the interface has already failed.**

### The Seven Rules Behind It

1. **Nothing hidden.** If a function exists, it has its own visible button. No swipe-to-reveal, no invisible edges, no multi-finger gestures. You tap the target and it happens.
2. **Nothing moves.** Controls never reorder, hide or shift. A room or a source always lives at the same spot on the glass, so the hand builds muscle memory instead of hunting. No screen ever scrolls.
3. **Colour before words.** State changes show first as colour and light, so you know what happened before you read a word.
4. **Built for real hands.** Every target is at least 44 px, with room around it to prevent misfires.
5. **One flat surface.** Where you are, what is playing and the volume sit together on one layer. No digging through folders.
6. **Safety nets in plain sight.** HUSH silences the whole house in one tap. BACK TO SQUARE ONE resets everything, with UNDO. Both are always visible.
7. **Honest by design.** The panel shows only what it can actually do. No dead toggles, no phantom sliders, no empty places that look like buttons.

## What's New Since v2.0

### 🔴 Dots That Explain the House
Each room is a coloured dot, the same on the panel, on PLAYING NOW and on Home:
- **White centre:** the room is in a group.
- **Coloured ring:** which group it belongs to, in the colour of the room the group plays from. Two groups, two ring colours.
- **Soft glow:** music is playing there.

### ⬛ The L-Shaped Now Playing Bar
What's playing runs along the bottom and rises into the corner beside the transport, where it holds **GROUP**: group *this* music with other rooms. A thin rainbow outline in the room colours flows slowly while music plays and stays still when the room is quiet.

### 🔊 Group Volume
The group page has its own **GROUP VOLUME**: big minus and plus that move every room in the group together, in fine steps, keeping their balance.

### 🏠 Home Comes First
The app opens with a short splash, a white SONOS on black that glides into the corner sign, and always lands on **Home**: every room, what it is playing, a MUTE for each one, and a big **HUSH** for the whole house.

### 💾 It Remembers
Groups, music, volumes, mutes, HUSH, sleep timers and the command log survive a reload, an app switch or closing the browser. BACK TO SQUARE ONE is the clean start.

### 📌 One Fixed Sign
The SONOS sign sits in the same corner on every screen. On the panel it opens Home; everywhere else it takes you back.

## Core Mechanics

### 🎨 Colour as Immediate Context
Every room has its own colour, and the panel takes on the colour of the room you're controlling. Recognising a colour is faster than reading a label, so people know where they are before they read a single word.

### 🎛️ Zero-Scroll Grouping
Grouping happens on one screen. The whole house fits in one glance, and "this room only" or "all rooms together" is one decision. **IN GROUP** in the header always shows how many rooms play together.

### ↩️ Mistakes Are Cheap
- **HUSH (Priority One):** silences the entire house instantly. RESUME brings back everything it can verify and names any room someone else changed meanwhile.
- **BACK TO SQUARE ONE:** asks first, resets every room to a clean state, then offers **UNDO**.

### 🗣️ Natural Multimodal Control
- **Voice:** speak a command and the panel answers out loud, with typed and tap fallbacks. Voice is never the only way in.
- **Intercom:** send an announcement; music in the chosen rooms lowers while you speak, then comes back on its own.
- **Ambient logic:** sleep timers fade music out gradually, and a morning alarm wakes the house.

### 🏗️ For the Professional Installation
- **Amp Multi zones:** when Kitchen and Dining are wired as one zone, the panel shows **Kitchen + Dining** with one volume.
- **Speakers that move:** carry a portable speaker next to the soundbar and it becomes a rear surround; the panel shows where it went.
- **Listeners, not just rooms:** headphones belong to a person, and HUSH leaves them alone.
- **Installer view:** a read-only map of the Amp Multi's outputs, the Sonos zones they form and the areas the family sees.
- **Saturday demo:** eight steps from 5:45 pm to 10:33 pm: dinner, guests, movie night, headphones, HUSH, another controller acting, and an honest RESUME.

### 🧭 Built for Trust
- **Command log:** a record of every action, including who acted (in Settings → HOUSE)
- **Lighting is a setting:** with no lighting system connected, those tiles become SLEEP and ALARM instead of pretending
- **Guided setup** for new speakers, clearly marked as sample devices
- **Sample houses:** 4-room, 6-room and Amp Multi homes for demonstration

## Why It Works

| Common pain point | The SONOS Panel's answer |
|---|---|
| "Which room am I controlling?" | The whole screen is that room's colour. |
| "Which rooms are grouped?" | White centres and matching rings, on every screen |
| Grouping buried in menus | The whole house, one screen, one decision |
| Controls that move or hide | Every button stays in the same place; nothing scrolls |
| Fear of messing things up | HUSH with honest RESUME, and UNDO on every reset |
| Two rooms wired as one zone | One linked area, one volume, clearly labelled |
| A speaker moved and the sound changed | The panel shows where it went and its new role |
| Several apps and voices controlling the house | RESUME respects their changes and says who made them |
| Losing your place after switching apps | The panel remembers the house exactly |

## Local First

Many of the homes this concept serves are exactly where the internet is least reliable: mountain and rural properties, second homes, boats, and owners who keep devices off the internet for privacy. The house has to keep playing when the outside connection fails.

The proposed path is a small hub inside the house that talks to the speakers over the local network, with the cloud as the online path for remote and account features. Sonos 27mcp shows the cloud side opening up to any controller; the local side is what keeps a luxury home working when the connection drops. This prototype does not yet simulate an internet outage; showing what still works when the connection drops is the next step, and a real local path still has to be proven in the field.

## Technical Execution

- A single self-contained file written in HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript, with no frameworks or libraries and no build step (typography loads from Google Fonts)
- Phone-native layout that fits every screen without scrolling, from small Android phones to large iPhones
- Saves its state on the device, in three layers, so it survives reloads even where browser storage is restricted
- Voice features depend on the browser's speech support, which varies by browser and device
- Add `?demo` to the address to open straight into the Saturday demo
- **All music, lighting, shade, speaker, headphone and network behaviour is simulated.** This is an interface concept and does not connect to real speakers or smart-home devices.

### Tested
- **Layout:** 24 screens on three phone sizes. Nothing scrolls, nothing is cut off, no button is under 44 px or covered by another element.
- **Controls:** every button on the panel tapped and checked for its result.
- **Logic:** an automated "robot user" taps hundreds of random controls in random order, as fast as an impatient person, on four phone sizes, and checks after every tap that groups, counts, dots, mute buttons, HUSH, volumes, UNDO and reload all stay true.
